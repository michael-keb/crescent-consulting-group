# Crescent Consulting Group — Client Requirements Change Breakdown

## Document purpose

This is a client-ready breakdown of the requested website changes from the WhatsApp voice-note transcript dated **13 September 2026**.

It should be reviewed alongside the current website:

**[Open the current Crescent Consulting Group website](https://common-ground-workplaces.mkeb.chatgpt.site)**

The current website is a public website preview. It includes the homepage, support pages, About page, community pathway, resources and FAQs, privacy and accessibility pages, and a conversation form. This document describes the requested direction and the changes to apply to the website; it does not treat the voice-note transcript as a replacement for the original website brief.

## Executive summary

The client wants the website to feel broader, more recognisably Australian and less explicitly religious in its first impression. The organisation should still retain its cultural understanding and bridge role, but the homepage should speak first to practical workplace outcomes and diverse communities.

The main changes are:

1. Broaden the hero language from a Muslim and Arab-specific framing to a wider communities-and-workplaces framing.
2. Make **“You don’t need all the answers, just where to start”** a prominent reassurance near the top of the page.
3. Replace narrow service labels with broader language about diverse workplaces and practical support.
4. Remove the “Faith at work” resource because it feels too preachy.
5. Replace the current hero image with a more typical Australian workplace image.
6. Combine the “Explore how we can help” treatment into one larger, clearer action.
7. Reduce the homepage length so the main story can be understood in approximately three natural scrolls.

## Detailed requirements

### 1. Hero eyebrow and positioning

#### Current

> UNDERSTANDING PEOPLE. CONNECTING WORKPLACES.

#### Requested change

> UNDERSTANDING PEOPLE. CONNECTING COMMUNITIES AND WORKPLACES.

#### Purpose

The client wants Crescent Consulting Group to be understood as connecting people, communities and workplaces. The phrase should feel inclusive of community relationships as well as employer relationships.

#### Acceptance criteria

- The revised phrase is visible in the first screen on desktop and mobile.
- “Communities and workplaces” is clearly readable and not hidden in a tooltip, image or secondary page.
- The wording does not make the organisation sound like a government body, religious authority or advocacy platform.

### 2. Hero heading

#### Requested direction

The client suggested:

> Understanding people, thriving workplaces.

The current heading is:

> Better understanding. Better together.

#### Requirement

Confirm one final hero heading before final content sign-off. The proposed client wording should be treated as the preferred option because it connects understanding directly to a workplace outcome.

#### Recommended final treatment

> Understanding people.  
> Thriving workplaces.

The heading should remain concise, calm and easy to scan. It should not become a long explanatory sentence.

### 3. Hero bridge sentence

#### Current

> A practical bridge between Australian workplaces and Muslim and Arab communities.

#### Requested change

> A practical bridge between Australian workplaces and Australian Muslim and diverse Middle Eastern communities.

#### Content note

This is the most specific wording proposed in the transcript. It should be confirmed with the client because “Australian Muslim and diverse Middle Eastern communities” may be intentionally precise, while the earlier suggestion “diverse communities” is broader.

#### Acceptance criteria

- The approved final wording appears in the homepage hero.
- The website does not use multiple competing versions of this sentence across the homepage and About page.
- The sentence continues to position Crescent Consulting Group as a practical bridge, not a representative of every community.

### 4. Hero reassurance and starting point

#### Current

> You don’t need all the answers. Just a place to start.

#### Requested change

Bring the reassurance closer to the top of the page and make it visually stand out:

> You don’t need all the answers, just where to start.

#### Layout requirement

The line should sit directly beneath or very close to the main hero heading and supporting sentence. It may be styled as a larger supporting statement or a highlighted side panel. It should not be buried beneath the main call to action.

#### Acceptance criteria

- A new visitor can see the reassurance without scrolling on a standard laptop viewport.
- The wording is visually distinct from ordinary body copy.
- It reinforces the conversation-first journey and reduces anxiety about choosing the right service.

### 5. Primary and secondary calls to action

#### Current treatment

The homepage has a primary **“Let’s start a conversation”** button and a separate **“Explore how we can help”** text link with an arrow.

#### Requested change

Combine “Explore how we can help” and its arrow/supporting treatment into one larger, clearer action area. The client also suggested bringing the line and the content beneath it together in one box.

#### Requirement

- Retain the conversation as the most prominent action.
- Make “Explore how we can help” feel like one deliberate action rather than a small link separated from its visual treatment.
- Keep the destination as the existing support overview page.
- Do not introduce a service-selection wizard or additional form step.

### 6. Audience list

#### Current

> Employers & HR teams · Schools & educators · Community & cultural teams

#### Requested presentation

> For the people who bring people together

1. Employers and HR teams
2. Schools and Educators
3. Community and Cultural Teams

#### Acceptance criteria

- The audience groups are easy to scan as a list.
- The requested capitalisation is applied: **Educators**, **Community**, **Cultural**, **Teams**.
- The audience strip remains visible early on the homepage.
- The list continues to make employers, schools, educators and cultural teams the primary audience.

### 7. Support-card terminology

#### Requested removals

Remove these labels from the homepage and primary navigation:

- “Muslim-friendly workplaces”
- “Muslim HR support group”

The transcript refers to “Muslim HR support” in one place and “Muslim HR support group” in another. The intent is clear: the client wants less narrow and less explicitly Muslim-labelled service language in the first impression.

#### Recommended replacements

- **Diverse workplace support**
- **Practical inclusion at work**
- **Cultural misunderstandings**
- **Respectful engagement**

#### Content preservation

The underlying service content should not be deleted. The HR, workplace inclusion, misunderstanding and engagement information can remain on the “How we help” page, but the labels should be reframed in broader language.

#### Acceptance criteria

- The homepage does not use “Muslim-friendly workplaces” or “Muslim HR support group.”
- The homepage does not appear to offer religious instruction.
- The deeper support page still explains that the organisation understands Muslim and Middle Eastern workplace contexts where relevant.
- The revised language does not erase the cultural expertise that makes the organisation useful.

### 9. About page portrait replacement

#### Requested change

Replace the existing About-page workplace photograph with the supplied portrait of the Crescent Consulting Group team member.

#### Implemented treatment

- Added the supplied portrait as the About-page image asset.
- Kept the existing rounded-corner frame so the new image fits the established visual system.
- Tuned the crop position to keep the subject’s face and upper body visible within the wide desktop frame and the shorter mobile frame.
- Set the image to load immediately so the frame does not appear blank while the page is first displayed.
- Kept the caption: “Room for a conversation. Space for another perspective.”

#### Acceptance criteria

- The supplied portrait is visible on the [About page](https://common-ground-workplaces.mkeb.chatgpt.site/about).
- The image remains aligned to the right of the bridge-on-conversation copy on desktop.
- The subject remains recognisable after responsive cropping on smaller screens.
- The image does not replace or disrupt the page heading, body copy or conversation link.

### 8. Resource library and “Faith at work”

#### Current

The resource library includes a guide titled:

> Making room for faith at work

#### Requested change

Remove, rename or replace this resource because the client described it as “preachy.”

#### Recommended replacement direction

Replace it with a neutral workplace guide such as:

> Making space for different needs at work

Possible supporting copy:

> Practical starting points for a workplace where people can contribute comfortably and openly.

The underlying practical content about breaks, food, shared spaces and employee preferences can be retained if it is rewritten in neutral workplace language.

#### Acceptance criteria

- “Faith at work” does not appear as a featured resource title.
- The resource does not read as religious instruction.
- The replacement remains useful for employers, HR teams and managers.
- The resource includes a reminder that individual preferences vary and people should be asked directly.

### 9. Human-first section

#### Current meaning

The section explains that workplaces work better when people understand each other and that Crescent Consulting Group connects employers with cultural perspectives and practical conversations.

#### Requested copy addition

Add:

> ...without preaching, and practical conversations that help make that possible.

#### Recommended complete copy

> Workplaces work better when people understand each other. We connect employers with cultural perspectives, without preaching, and practical conversations that help make that possible.

#### Acceptance criteria

- “Without preaching” is present in the approved final copy, or an equivalent phrase is approved by the client.
- The wording remains positive and professional rather than defensive.
- The section continues to explain the practical value of the organisation.

### 10. Hero image

#### Current issue

The current image shows a highly visible culturally diverse meeting, including Muslim representation. The client feels that it may be too overtly Muslim-focused and does not resemble the average Australian workplace closely enough.

#### Requested direction

Use a typical Australian workplace scene:

- Everyday workplace setting.
- Natural, candid interaction.
- Balanced diversity without making cultural identity the only visual message.
- Less emphasis on visible scarves, religious markers or a staged cultural consultation scene.
- A calm, credible and practical feeling.

#### Usage requirements

- The image must be properly licensed for website use.
- The image must not imply that the pictured people are Crescent Consulting Group staff, clients or endorsers.
- The image location must not be described as Australian unless verified.
- Alt text should describe the scene rather than make assumptions about identity.

### 11. Homepage length and scrolling

#### Requested outcome

The homepage should be easier to understand within approximately three natural scrolls.

#### Recommended changes

- Tighten vertical spacing between the hero, audience strip and support section.
- Keep the reassurance close to the hero rather than repeating it later.
- Reduce repeated explanations of the conversation-first approach.
- Keep the support cards concise.
- Keep the resource section to a compact preview with a clear link to the library.
- Preserve one strong conversation banner near the end of the page.

#### Acceptance criteria

- A visitor can understand the organisation, audience, support areas and next action in approximately three scroll lengths on desktop.
- Mobile remains readable without horizontal overflow.
- Important content is not removed solely to shorten the page; it is condensed or moved to the relevant supporting page.

- Removing all references to Muslim, Arab or Middle Eastern communities from the deeper site.
- Introducing new services, credentials, testimonials, partner logos or certifications.
- Making claims about Australian photography locations without verification.
